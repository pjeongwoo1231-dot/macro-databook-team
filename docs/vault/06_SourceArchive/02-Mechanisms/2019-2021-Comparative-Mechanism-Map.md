## Core chain

> pre-Covid financial-cycle expansion and dollar debt → pandemic sudden stop → dollar funding stress and portfolio-flow reversal → asset-manager and bank balance-sheet pressure → fiscal–monetary support and central-bank liquidity → recovery with uneven inflation and asset-price rebound → policy-normalisation and capital-flow reversal risk

## Analytical distinctions

The sources differ in evidentiary status. BIS, IMF, OECD, and World Bank materials primarily provide institutional diagnosis, survey evidence, or forecasts. NBER, BOJ, and Fed papers provide empirical or model-based analysis, but working papers and FEDS notes remain preliminary. The BOJ QQE estimate is a model counterfactual, while the Fed paper uses geographic separation of foreign Covid exposure and domestic lending to identify a credit-supply channel. The OECD paper's monthly dataset improves timing relative to quarterly balance-of-payments data.

## Relation to later periods

The 2019–2021 set supplies the baseline for comparing 2022–2023 inflation and tightening, and 2024–2026 policy uncertainty, sovereign/NBFI risk, and geopolitical supply shocks. The key comparison is whether the later period reverses the pandemic-era policy support through a financial-conditions shock, rather than through the initial real-economy lockdown shock.
