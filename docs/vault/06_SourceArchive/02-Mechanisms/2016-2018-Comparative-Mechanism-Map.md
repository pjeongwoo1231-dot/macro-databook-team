# 2016–2018 Comparative Mechanism Map

## Core chain

> post-GFC low rates and search for yield → credit/property/financial-cycle expansion → high public/private and EME dollar debt → policy normalisation and political/market repricing → bank–non-bank–sovereign feedback → narrow macroprudential and monetary policy space

## Mechanism table

| Stage | Transmission | Evidence in sources |
|---|---|---|
| Financial-cycle build-up | Low rates and accommodative global financial conditions lift asset valuations, credit and debt. | [[BIS-AER-2016]] [[BIS-AER-2018]] |
| External spillovers | US dollar, global capital flows, exchange rates and international monetary conditions transmit shocks across EMEs. | [[BIS-AER-2016]] [[BIS-AER-2017]] |
| Normalisation risk | Rising rates, balance-sheet unwinding and dollar strength expose debt-service and refinancing vulnerabilities. | [[BIS-AER-2017]] [[BIS-AER-2018]] |
| Bank–non-bank amplification | Institutional investors, funds, repo markets and banks interact during yield snapbacks and liquidity stress. | [[BIS-AER-2018]] |
| Sovereign–financial nexus | Sovereign exposures and fiscal conditions can transmit stress to banks, while banking-sector weakness can return to the sovereign. | [[BIS-AER-2016]] |
| Policy trade-off | Monetary policy cannot simultaneously stabilise inflation, output, exchange rates and financial cycles; macroprudential and fiscal tools must complement it. | [[BIS-AER-2016]] [[BIS-AER-2018]] |

## Link to 2019–2021

The 2016–2018 reports describe the balance-sheet and policy conditions that made the 2020 shock unusually financialised. The later chain is therefore not a discontinuity: pre-existing debt, global dollar funding, non-bank intermediation and limited policy room became the amplification layer for the pandemic sudden stop. The 2019–2021 package then documents the shock, liquidity response, bank-lending spillover, capital-flow reversal and normalisation risks.
